# Pick Up / Put Down Settling Technique

**Date Created:** February 9, 2026
**Status:** Active
**Source:** 5-12 Month Sleep Course, Module 3

## Overview

The Pick Up / Put Down (PU/PD) method is a responsive settling technique that provides maximum hands-on support while teaching your baby to fall asleep independently. This method is ideal for parents who want to stay close and provide immediate physical reassurance throughout the settling process.

**Best suited for:**
- Parents who prefer a more hands-on approach
- Babies who respond well to physical comfort
- Families transitioning from high contact sleep associations
- Parents who find it difficult to hear any crying without immediate response

**Age range:** 5-12 months

---

## The Pick Up / Put Down Method: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Put Baby Down Awake

You will put your baby down in the bassinet, after a pre-bed/nap routine awake (or drowsy if need be).

### Step 2: Offer Immediate Reassurance (If Crying Starts)

If your baby starts to cry you are going to put your hand on them and give them your touch, voice and closeness for at least 1 minute. You can shush them or sing to them, or quietly talk to them. Your job is to ooze calmness.

### Step 3: Assess the Situation (After 1-2 Minutes)

After 1-2 minutes have passed, assess the situation. Is your baby's crying escalating and becoming more and more emotional? Or perhaps they are starting to only grizzle and settle and crying is becoming inconsistent.

### Step 4: Pick Up (If Crying Escalates)

If their crying is escalating you will pick them up and settle them in your arms.

### Step 5: Soothe and Calm

You can pat their back and shush in their ear. If they have a strong feed-to-sleep association, you can breastfeed them quickly 1-2 times per pu/pd session to help with the calming process.

### Step 6: Put Back Down (When Calm, Not Asleep)

When your baby has calmed down (not asleep) and is ready to go back in the cot, put them back down on their back.

### Step 7: Repeat Until Asleep

Repeat steps 1-5 until baby is asleep, or for at least 20 minutes before fully assisting your baby to sleep.

## What to Expect

**Initial frequency:** The amount of times you need to pick up/put down will most likely diminish over the coming days to weeks. The goal is to wean out your involvement to allow your baby to self settle off to sleep. Initially, you may be picking up your baby 10-20 times in one settling session, and that's ok.

**Progress timeline:**
- **Nights 1-3:** High frequency of pick ups (10-20+ times per session is normal)
- **Days 4-7:** Gradual reduction in pick ups needed
- **Week 2:** Significant decrease in intervention required
- **Week 3-4:** Baby increasingly able to settle with minimal pick ups

**Common patterns:**
- Most babies calm quickly when picked up, but may start crying again when put down initially
- This is normal and part of the learning process
- Consistency is key, don't switch methods partway through
- You should see progressive improvement, even if gradual

## Key Principles

### 1. Calm, Not Asleep

The critical distinction: you put your baby back down when they're CALM, not when they're asleep. This is what teaches them to make the final transition to sleep independently.

### 2. Consistency Builds Trust

Your baby learns to trust the pattern: cry → pick up → calm → put down → repeat. This predictability helps them feel secure even as they learn a new skill.

### 3. Ooze Calmness

Your energy matters. Stay calm, move slowly, keep your voice quiet and soothing. Your baby takes cues from you.

### 4. Feed-to-Sleep Association

If your baby has a strong feed-to-sleep association, you can breastfeed quickly 1-2 times per pu/pd session to help with the calming process. This bridges the gap while you're breaking the association.

### 5. The 20-Minute Rule

Repeat the process for at least 20 minutes before fully assisting your baby to sleep. This gives them adequate opportunity to practice the new skill while ensuring they don't become overtired.

## Overnight Wakings

Use the same method for overnight wakings:

1. **Rule out hunger:** If it's a scheduled feed time, feed first, then use PU/PD to resettle if needed
2. **Start with presence:** Put hand on baby, offer voice and touch
3. **Assess:** Give 1-2 minutes to see if they settle with just your presence
4. **Pick up if needed:** If crying escalates, pick up and calm
5. **Put down calm:** Return to cot when calm (not asleep)
6. **Repeat:** Continue for at least 20 minutes before fully assisting

**Night wakings typically improve faster than bedtime** because your baby has already learned the skill at bedtime.

---

## Early Morning Wakings

If your baby wakes before 6:00 AM:

1. **Use the same PU/PD process** - don't treat early morning differently
2. **Don't start the day** - keep room dark, stay calm and boring
3. **At least 20 minutes:** Give your baby at least 20 minutes to resettle before getting up for the day
4. **Full assist if needed:** If they don't resettle after 20 minutes, you can fully assist back to sleep or start the day (based on how close to wake time)

**Early morning wakings are often the last to resolve** - be patient and consistent.

## When PU/PD is the Right Choice

### Choose this method if:
- You want maximum hands-on involvement in the settling process
- Your baby responds well to being held and soothed
- You're transitioning from co-sleeping or contact napping
- The idea of leaving the room feels too difficult right now
- You have the time and energy for a more intensive method (at least initially)

### Consider a different method if:
- You find the repetitive picking up/putting down physically exhausting
- Your baby becomes MORE upset when picked up and put down repeatedly
- You prefer a method with more structure and predictability (try Soothe and Support Checks)
- You want to create more physical distance (try The Vanishing Chair)

## Troubleshooting

### "My baby cries harder when I pick them up"

Some babies find the pattern of pick up/put down frustrating rather than soothing. If your baby's crying intensifies with this method after 2-3 nights, try Soothe and Support Checks or The Vanishing Chair instead.

### "I'm picking up 30+ times and we're not making progress"

Make sure you're putting baby down when CALM, not asleep. If you're waiting until they're drowsy or asleep, they're not getting the practice they need. Also ensure you're giving at least 1-2 minutes of in-cot soothing before picking up.

### "My arms are exhausted"

This is physically demanding, especially in the first few days. Make sure you're using good posture and consider whether The Vanishing Chair might be a better fit if the physical demand is unsustainable.

### "My baby falls asleep in my arms every time"

Put them down SOONER - as soon as they're calm, even if still grizzling slightly. The goal is not to get them drowsy in your arms, but to calm the escalated crying so they can practice settling in the cot.

### "We're on night 5 and it's not getting better"

The "night 4 or 5 wobble" is real. Many families see progress nights 1-3, then hit a rough patch nights 4-6 before improvement continues. Don't switch methods in the wobble, push through with consistency.

## Important Notes

**Not sleep training without support:** This method still involves significant parental involvement. You're present, responsive, and providing hands-on comfort. You're teaching a skill with support, not leaving your baby to cry alone.

**Requires consistency:** Pick up/put down only works if you commit to the pattern. Switching to rocking to sleep or feeding to sleep partway through confuses your baby and extends the learning period.

**Part of a complete system:** Like all settling techniques, PU/PD works best when paired with:
- Age-appropriate wake windows and schedule
- A consistent pre-sleep routine
- A sleep-friendly environment (dark, cool, white noise)
- Clear rules about overnight feeds

**When to stop:** If after 3-4 nights you're seeing no progress, or your baby seems more distressed by the pattern, this method may not be the right fit. Try The Vanishing Chair or Soothe and Support Checks instead.

---

## Success Indicators

You'll know it's working when:
- The number of pick ups per session gradually decreases
- Your baby calms more quickly when picked up
- Your baby starts to settle with just your hand/voice (skipping the pick up)
- Overnight wakings decrease in frequency and duration
- Your baby goes down more calmly at bedtime
- You see a pattern of progressive improvement, even if not linear

**Remember:** Progress is rarely linear. You may have a great night followed by a rough one. Look at the overall trend over 5-7 days, not night-to-night variation.
