# Snooze Website Copy Review

This is the full body of copy currently live across the Snooze website, checkout flows, landing pages and email sequences, pulled from the source of truth and laid out for review. It is organised in customer-journey order: core website pages first, then age and product pages, campaign and paid landing pages, checkout pages, and the email sequences that follow each offer.

### How to review

- Read through each page and leave a comment (highlight the text, then Insert > Comment, or Ctrl+Alt+M) wherever something reads wrong, sounds off-brand, is out of date, or you want changed.
- The copy is reproduced as it currently appears live. If you spot a typo or awkward line, it is in the live copy, so flag it.
- Buttons are shown as **[Button: text]**. Merge fields like ((member.name)) and {{first_name}} are personalisation tokens that fill in automatically when the page or email is sent.
- You do not need to review the Legal & Policy and Shared Components sections for voice; check those for factual accuracy (names, dates, contact details, pricing) only.

## Flags raised while preparing this document

These points came up while laying out the copy. Nothing was changed; they are noted here so you can decide during your review.

- **Live coaching / live sessions language.** Several pages promise live sessions or live coaching (About Sally closing CTA, the Snooze Method explainer, the Founding Member landing page and its testimonials, and the 7-Day Trial thank-you page). This conflicts with the current service model, which does not offer weekly or live group coaching. Worth deciding what stays.
- **Sally's positioning wording.** A few sales pages describe Sally as "paediatric nurse" or "certified sleep consultant", and one uses present tense ("is a paediatric nurse"). The approved standard is "internationally certified sleep consultant and former paediatric nurse", with "former" always used. Affected: Snooze Method, Newborn Guide, 3-4 Month Course, Toddler Toolkit sales pages, and the Cold Traffic landing page.
- **Unfinished offer placeholder in emails.** The 1-Month Free Day 30 email and the 7-Day Trial trial-ended email still contain an unfilled placeholder: "[SPECIAL OFFER - e.g., $20 off your first payment...]". This looks like it was never finalised and would send as-is.
- **Blog Index is a template, not copy.** The Blog Index page is a placeholder layout with bracketed tokens ([POST_TITLE] etc.), not finished copy. It populates automatically from blog posts, so there is little to review there.
- **Minor duplicates in source.** The Camp Snooze waitlist landing repeats one FAQ answer and a CTA line, and the Snooze Access results page has a duplicated testimonial. These are reproduced as they appear live.

**Undertired**
Undertired is when your baby simply hasn't been awake long enough to build the sleep pressure they need. They look ready to you, but their body isn't there yet.

An undertired baby will usually play, chat or grizzle quietly in the cot rather than properly cry. It's easy to read as a sleep problem when the real answer is a slightly longer wake window. A little more awake time before the next sleep usually settles it quickly.

Related Sleep Pressure Wake Windows Split Night

**Melatonin**
Melatonin is the hormone the body releases as it gets dark to signal that it's time to sleep. It nudges your baby toward drowsiness and lowers alertness.

Newborns make very little of their own at first, which is part of why their sleep is all over the place early on. Bright light and screens in the evening hold it back, while dim, warm light lets it flow. It's why a dark room and a calm wind-down genuinely help, and why I'm such a fan of pitch black for day naps too.

###### Module 4: Settling Techniques

If your baby only falls asleep with feeding, rocking, or holding, this module shows you how to teach self-settling at 4 months old. You'll learn gentle settling techniques, when to start sleep training, and how to help your baby fall asleep independently. This addresses the core of the 4-month regression: babies who wake at the end of each sleep cycle and need help resettling the way they first fell asleep.

###### Module 5: Night Wakes & Feeding

Dealing with a 4-month-old waking every hour or multiple times at night? This module helps you understand whether night wakings are due to hunger, habit, or the sleep regression. You'll learn how to distinguish feeding needs from resettling needs, when overnight feeds are still necessary, and strategies to reduce frequent night wakings. Perfect for parents asking "how can I stop night wakings?" or "why does my baby wake every 2 hours?"

Frustrated with 30-minute cat naps? This module explains why short naps happen at 3-4 months and gives you practical techniques to help your baby sleep longer. You'll learn about sleep cycles, the Le Pause technique, and the Cot-Hour strategy to extend naps. Ideal for parents dealing with nap resistance, short daytime sleep, or wondering how to consolidate naps at this age.
