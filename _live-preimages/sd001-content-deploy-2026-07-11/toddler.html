Toddler sleep, in plain terms

Toddler sleep is less about sleep skills and more about development and boundaries. Bedtime resistance, early waking, nap changes and testing limits are all normal parts of this stage. Knowing what is developmental helps you decide when to hold a boundary and when to wait it out.

Consistency is what settles toddler sleep, calm and clear routines that hold even when your toddler pushes back. The guidance here is the evidence-based picture of what is normal after the first year, from an internationally certified sleep consultant and former paediatric nurse.

What's normal at this age

  • Testing boundaries at bedtime, including the extra request for water, another story or one more cuddle.
  • Dropping to one nap and eventually to none, usually somewhere between fifteen months and three years.
  • Early rising and bedtime resistance, often tied to nap timing and an overtired or undertired day.
  • New fears, big feelings and language leaps that can briefly disrupt settled sleep.

Normal, or something to address?

Situation Normal? First step When to get help
Extra requests at bedtime: water, another story, one more cuddle Normal boundary testing Hold a calm, predictable bedtime routine If bedtime is escalating into hours of distress most nights
Dropping the last nap somewhere between fifteen months and three years Normal nap transition Shift bedtime earlier on no-nap days If overtiredness is causing night waking and early rising for weeks
Bedtime resistance and early waking Often nap timing or an over or undertired day Adjust nap length and timing, then bedtime If a consistent routine over one to two weeks changes nothing
New fears or big feelings at bedtime Normal with language and developmental leaps Acknowledge the feeling and keep the boundary steady If fears are severe or persistent and affecting the daytime too
Waking earlier and earlier across the week Addressable drift Check the nap and bedtime for gradual changes If drift continues after ten days of steady routine
Sleep change with a fever or refusing food or fluids Not a sleep issue Check temperature and offer fluids Contact your GP for a fever over 38C, refusing fluids or signs of illness
