When feeds are anchored, you actually have *more* flexibility, not less. You know when they're hungry and when they're not. You can troubleshoot with confidence.

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: Understanding the 4-Month Changes**

Hi, I'm Sally. And I'm genuinely excited that you're here.

I know that sounds strange when you're running on no sleep and feeling like everything has broken. But hear me out.

The 4-month regression is not you failing. It's not your baby broken. It's actually a sign that your baby's brain is developing exactly as it should.

**What's actually happening**

Before this point, your baby slept in a very simple pattern: active sleep, quiet sleep, active sleep. Very newborn. Very sleepy.

Around 3-5 months, their sleep architecture matures. They now cycle through four stages of sleep, just like adults:

1. Drowsy/light sleep
2. Light sleep
3. Deep sleep
4. REM (dream sleep)

A full cycle takes about 40-50 minutes. At the end of each cycle, your baby briefly stirs. If they can't resettle themselves, they wake fully and call out for you.

**Why this feels like regression**

If your baby was being rocked, fed, or bounced to sleep, their brain was doing a little "bookmark" each time they drifted off: *This is how we fall asleep.*

Now, at the end of every sleep cycle, their brain checks for that same environment. If it's gone (you've tiptoed away, the rocking has stopped), they wake fully.

This is not manipulation. This is biology. And it's completely fixable.

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- The science behind why the 4-month sleep changes happen
- Why this is actually the perfect time to teach new skills
- What "self-settling" really means (and what it doesn't)

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

Understanding the regression is the first step. But teaching your baby to self-settle, adjusting their schedule, and anchoring feeds are all part of the solution.

# Variant 3: Self-Settling (LMCR13)

## Target Problem: No Independent Sleep
Parents whose baby cannot fall asleep without extensive hands-on assistance.

---

## Orientation Lesson

### Video Script (60-90 seconds)

**[Sally, warm but matter-of-fact, speaking to camera]**

Hi, I'm Sally, The Sleep Concierge.

If you've found this Free Module, I'm guessing you're in a very particular situation right now.

Your baby cannot fall asleep unless you are doing *something*. Rocking. Feeding. Bouncing. Pacing the hallway. Maybe all of the above, in rotation, for what feels like hours.

And once they finally sleep? They wake the moment you put them down. Or they sleep for one cycle and need you to start from scratch.

You're exhausted. And you're wondering if there's another way.

There absolutely is.

Self-settling is the ability for your baby to transition from awake to asleep, on their own, in their sleep space. It's not neglecting them. It's not "crying it out" in the scary way you might have heard. It's teaching them a skill.

Think of it this way: right now, you are your baby's only way of falling asleep. Every time they stir between sleep cycles, they need you because that's all they know. When you teach self-settling, you're giving them a second option. They can *also* put themselves back to sleep.

In this Free Module, I'm going to explain what self-settling really is, why it matters at this age, and the different techniques available. I'm not going to leave you in the dark.

Now, self-settling is one piece of the puzzle. At this age, feeds, environment, and schedule all play a role too. If you want the full plan, my 3-4 Month Sleep Course covers everything.

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: Settling Techniques**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I'm really glad you're here.

I know what it feels like to be your baby's only method of falling asleep. The rocking. The feeding. The bouncing on a fit ball until your legs burn. And then the horror of putting them down only to have those little eyes pop right back open.

You're not alone. And this is absolutely fixable.

**What is self-settling?**

Self-settling is your baby's ability to transition from awake to asleep without needing hands-on help from you.

It doesn't mean leaving them alone to cry for hours. It doesn't mean being harsh or ignoring their needs. It means teaching them a skill, so that they have another option besides "Mum or Dad does everything."

**Why does it matter at this age?**

Around 3-4 months, your baby's sleep cycles mature. They now briefly wake between cycles (about every 40-50 minutes). If they can only fall asleep with your help, they'll call for that same help every single cycle.

This is why you might be up 6, 8, 10 times a night. It's not hunger. It's habit.

When your baby learns to self-settle at bedtime, they can also resettle between cycles. That's how you get longer stretches.

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- What self-settling actually means (and what it doesn't)
- Why self-settling is the key to linking sleep cycles
- An overview of gentle and more structured settling techniques
- How to know which approach might suit your family

**This is not about "crying it out"**

There are many different ways to teach self-settling. Some involve more parental presence. Some involve less. All of them can work when done consistently.

In this module, I'll walk you through the options so you can make an informed choice.

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

Self-settling is a big piece. But at this age, feeds, environment, and schedule all matter too. This Free Module gives you the settling foundations. If you want the complete plan, my 3-4 Month Sleep Course puts everything together.
