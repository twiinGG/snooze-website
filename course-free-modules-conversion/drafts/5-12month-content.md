Your baby is waking frequently overnight, and you're wondering: is this hunger, or is this habit? Should I be feeding them? Am I creating a problem? Will they ever sleep through the night?

You're not alone. This is one of the most common questions I get.

Here's the truth. Between 5-12 months, most healthy, growing babies are developmentally capable of sleeping longer stretches without needing a feed. They may still have one or two legitimate overnight feeds, depending on their age. But for many babies, those 4, 5, 6+ feeds overnight have become habit, not hunger.

The difference matters. Because if you're feeding every time they wake, you're reinforcing the pattern. They wake, they eat. Wake, eat. Wake, eat. Their body clock learns to expect food at 11pm, 1am, 3am, 5am. And they keep waking to get it.

In this Free Module, I'm going to help you understand what's actually going on. What feeds are reasonable at this age. And how to start gently reducing overnight feeds if that's what's right for your family.

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: Feeding & Night Weaning**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I'm so glad you're here.

You've found this module because overnight feeds are ruling your life. Maybe you're up 4, 5, 6 times a night. Maybe you're exhausted beyond words. Maybe you're wondering if your baby actually *needs* all this milk or if it's become something else entirely.

Let me help you figure it out.

**The hunger vs habit question**

Between 5-12 months, most healthy babies are capable of longer overnight stretches without food. That doesn't mean they'll magically do it. It means the *need* for constant feeding has often shifted to *habit*.

Signs that overnight feeds may be habit:
- Your baby doesn't seem genuinely hungry (feeds are quick, distracted)
- They're eating well during the day
- Weight gain is normal
- They're waking at the same predictable times every night

Signs that overnight feeds are still needed:
- Your baby is still young (5-6 months especially)
- Weight gain has been a concern
- They're genuinely hungry and feeding well overnight
- Your health professional has advised continued night feeds

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- What's normal for overnight feeds at each age
- How to tell if a wake is hunger or habit
- Strategies for gently reducing overnight feeds
- How anchoring day feeds can reduce night feeds naturally

**This isn't about starving anyone**

I want to be really clear. We are never taking calories away from your baby. We're redistributing them. When day feeds are well-anchored and satisfying, overnight feeds naturally become less necessary.

If your baby needs to eat, you feed them. But if they're waking out of habit, you can gently change the pattern.

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

Feeds are a big piece, but self-settling, schedule, and environment all matter too. This Free Module tackles the feeding piece. If you want the complete toolkit, my 5-12 Month Sleep Guide has everything.

### Video Script (60-90 seconds)

**[Sally, empathetic but confident, speaking to camera]**

Hi, I'm Sally, The Sleep Concierge.

If you've found this Free Module, I'm guessing you're dealing with the classic catnapper. 30 minutes. 40 minutes if you're lucky. And then those little eyes pop open and the nap is done.

You've tried everything. Dark room, white noise, perfect temperature. You've timed the nap perfectly. And still, that 35-minute mark arrives and boom. Done.

I hear you. Catnapping is one of the most frustrating sleep challenges at this age.

Here's what's going on.

Your baby is waking at the end of one sleep cycle. That cycle is about 40-50 minutes long. At the end of it, they briefly stir. And instead of drifting into another cycle, they wake fully. Often loudly.

The question is: why?

Usually, it comes down to one of two things. Either they don't know how to resettle themselves between cycles. Or the timing and conditions aren't quite right.

In this Free Module, I'm going to walk you through nap training. How to help your baby link those cycles, so you get proper, restorative naps instead of tiny broken fragments.

Now, naps are connected to everything else. Night settling, schedule, feeds, they all play a role. If you want the complete system, my 5-12 Month Sleep Guide covers it all.

But for now? Let's solve that catnapping.

---

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: Nap Training / Solving Catnapping**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I completely understand the frustration that brought you here.

Catnapping is one of those challenges that can break even the most patient parent. You finally get your baby down. You tiptoe away. You have approximately 32 minutes of freedom. And then... they're awake. Again.

Let's fix this.

**Why do babies catnap?**

At the end of each sleep cycle (roughly 40-50 minutes), your baby briefly stirs. Most babies who sleep longer stretches don't magically skip this, they just resettle and drift into the next cycle without fully waking.

Catnapping babies can't do this yet. They hit that 40-minute mark, stir, and wake fully.

The reasons usually fall into two buckets:
1. **They don't know how to resettle.** If they were bounced, fed, or rocked to sleep, their brain expects that same help to get back to sleep.
2. **Timing or conditions aren't quite right.** Overtiredness, undertiredness, or environmental issues can make resettling harder.

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- The science of why catnapping happens
- How to teach your baby to link sleep cycles
- Nap training techniques (with options for different comfort levels)
- When to rescue a nap vs. when to let it go
- The "assisted days, independent nights" approach

**My mantra: Assisted days, independent nights**

Here's something I want you to know. You don't have to nap train all naps at once. In fact, I often recommend focusing on nights first, then tackling naps one at a time.

You can even use contact naps or pram naps strategically to protect the schedule while you work on independent settling elsewhere.

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

Naps don't exist in isolation. Schedule, night settling, environment, and feeds all affect daytime sleep.

This Free Module gives you the nap training foundations. If you want the complete toolkit, my 5-12 Month Sleep Guide connects all the pieces.

Ready to conquer catnapping? Press play on the next lesson.
