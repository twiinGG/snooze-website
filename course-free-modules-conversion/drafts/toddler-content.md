### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: The 18-Month Regression**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I'm so glad you're here.

If you're reading this, there's a good chance you've just experienced the shock of a previously good sleeper suddenly refusing everything about bedtime. The protests. The demands. The screaming the moment you leave the room.

Welcome to the 18-month regression.

**What's actually happening**

The 18-month regression is a very real wobble, even for excellent sleepers. It usually shows up somewhere between 15-18 months and often peaks for around 2-6 weeks.

This phase is less about broken sleep skills and more about development. Toddlers are:
- Testing limits and figuring out their power
- Flexing new language (suddenly "more book" and "mummy stay" are possible)
- Working out how much control they have in their little world

**How it shows up**

- Sudden crying or screaming the moment you leave
- Demands for more of everything: more books, more cuddles, more water
- Jumping, shouting, shaking the cot rails
- A quick 10-minute bedtime blowing out to an hour or more

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- Why this regression happens (the developmental picture)
- How to maintain your bedtime routine
- The doorway response plan (calm, boring, consistent)
- Common traps that make the regression drag on
- Daytime strategies that support nights

**You are the unflappable leader**

Here's a line you can borrow from me: "I am their unflappable leader."

You can feel unsure on the inside and still act calm on the outside. Toddlers read our energy far more than our words. When you are clear, consistent, and calm, it helps them relax into the new pattern more quickly.

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

The 18-month regression is one piece. Nap transitions, schedules, and settling strategies all connect. This Free Module gives you regression-specific tools. If you want the complete toddler toolkit, it's all inside my Toddler Toolkit course.

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: The 2-1 Nap Drop & Transitional Schedule**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I know exactly why you're here.

The 2-1 nap transition is confusing. Your toddler is sending mixed signals. Some days two naps seem essential. Other days the second nap is a complete battle. You're not sure if you should drop a nap, rescue a nap, or just wait it out.

Let me give you some clarity.

**When is it actually time for one nap?**

Most toddlers drop to one nap somewhere between 14-18 months. But age isn't the only indicator. Signs your toddler might be ready:

- You're already on the Transitional Schedule (short morning nap, long afternoon nap)
- The morning nap is capped at 30 minutes and has been for a while
- They consistently refuse the morning nap even with quick-win methods
- Their afternoons aren't falling apart

**The biggest mistake: Dropping too early**

Here's what I see constantly. Parents drop to one nap because the second nap gets refused. But the real problem is usually that the **first nap is too long**.

A long morning nap front-loads all the sleep pressure. By afternoon, your toddler isn't tired enough to nap. Then they hit bedtime overtired.

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- The Transitional Schedule: Short morning nap (30 min), long afternoon nap (2 hours)
- How to run each nap strategically
- Signs that it's genuinely time for one nap
- What to do on a "no morning nap" day
- Troubleshooting common 2-1 problems

**Going back to two naps is sometimes smart**

You're not going backward if you briefly go back to a second nap as a rescue. Reduce the cortisol. Get everyone rested. Then try again.

**Sleep is a holistic puzzle**

Nap transitions don't exist in isolation. Regressions, settling strategies, and schedule adjustments all connect. This Free Module gives you the 2-1 framework. If you want the complete toddler toolkit, it's all in my Toddler Toolkit course.

### Lesson Text Body

**Welcome to Your Free Module: The 2-Year Regression**

Hi, I'm Sally, and I completely understand the chaos that brought you here.

The 2-year regression is intense. Your toddler was napping. They were sleeping at night. And then, almost overnight, everything fell apart. Nap refusal. Bedtime battles. A small human who has suddenly developed an *extremely* strong opinion about sleep.

Let me help you understand what's happening.

**What is the 2-year regression?**

Around the second birthday, many toddlers go through a major developmental wobble. Language is exploding. Autonomy is huge. They're discovering that they can say "no" and mean it.

This shows up as:
- Nap refusal (even when they clearly need it)
- Bedtime battles that drag on for hours
- Demands for you to stay, lie down, or never leave
- A sense that your previously good sleeper has completely disappeared

**The critical truth: Most 2-year-olds still need a nap**

This is the most important thing I can tell you. Just because your toddler is refusing a nap doesn't mean they're done with naps.

The nap usually drops somewhere between 2 years 9 months and 3+ years. At 2 years old, your toddler almost certainly still needs that midday rest.

What you're seeing is *behaviour*, not readiness.

**What you'll learn in this module:**

- Why the 2-year regression happens
- The nap training protocol (consistent cot time, every day)
- Rescue plans when everyone is fried
- Avoiding new sleep props during this phase
- How to know when the nap is *actually* on the way out

**Your mantra: "You are their unflappable leader"**

This phase requires patience, consistency, and calm. Your toddler is allowed to be upset about sleep. You are allowed to hold the boundary around sleep. Both can exist.
