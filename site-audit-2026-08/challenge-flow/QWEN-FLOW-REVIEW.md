# Snooze Sleep-Challenge Pages: IA and Conversion Review

## 1. Shared Page Sequence and Variants

The six pages follow a rigid, identical structural template. This consistency is the primary driver of the "interchangeable" feeling.

**Standard Sequence (5 of 6 pages):**
1.  **Header/Nav:** Shared chrome.
2.  **H1 Title:** `[Challenge]: why [subject] [problem] and what to do first`
3.  **Subhead/Lead:** 2-3 sentences defining the issue and the "first check."
4.  **Author/Review Line:** "Written by/Reviewed by Sally Woods..."
5.  **Section: What's going on:** Mechanism explanation + "On the consult floor" trap.
6.  **Section: Is this normal?:** Three sub-bullets (Usually normal / Worth working on / When to get help/GP).
7.  **Section: First steps you can take today:** Four bullet points (Log/Track, Check timing/schedule, Specific fix, Rule out/Protect).
8.  **Section: Free [Resource Name]:** Pitch for a specific free asset (Toolkit, Guide, Module, Masterclass).
9.  **Section: Related sleep help:** Three links to other challenge pages.
10. **Section: All the help, in one place:** Membership pitch (7-day trial).
11. **Footer:** Shared chrome.

**Variant: Sleep Regressions**
This page breaks the template by inserting an additional section between the Free Resource and the Membership pitch:
*   **Extra Section: Free help for the regression you are in:** A sub-navigation block splitting advice by age (4 months vs. 18 months/2 years).
*   *Impact:* This makes the page feel longer and more complex, but it also disrupts the linear flow, potentially confusing the "one clear next step" journey.

**Variant: Catnapping**
This page includes an **FAQ Section** ("Catnapping FAQs") after "First steps" and before the Free Resource.
*   *Impact:* This adds significant length and repetition, as the FAQ answers largely restate the "What's going on" and "First steps" content.

## 2. Quantification of Repetition

The repetition is structural and lexical, not just thematic.

| Element | Frequency | Nature of Repetition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H1 Structure** | 6/6 | Identical syntax: `[Topic]: why [subject] [verb] and what to do first`. |
| **Lead Paragraph** | 6/6 | Follows pattern: Definition + "Normal vs. Problem" + "Check X before changing Y." |
| **"What's going on"** | 6/6 | Starts with mechanism. Ends with "On the consult floor, a common trap is..." |
| **"Is this normal?"** | 6/6 | Identical sub-headers: *Usually normal*, *Worth working on*, *When to get help/GP*. |
| **"First steps"** | 6/6 | Identical 4-step logic: 1. Log/Track, 2. Check Timing/Schedule, 3. Specific Action, 4. Rule out/Protect. |

| **Related Help** | 6/6 | Three links, each with a short descriptor. |

| **Footer** | 6/6 | Identical. |

**Lexical Repetition:**
*   "On the consult floor" appears in all 6 "What's going on" sections.
*   "Log [X] for a week" or "Track the day" appears in all 6 "First steps" sections.
*   "Free, no card needed" appears in all 6 Free Resource sections.
*   "Snooze Membership brings every course... in one place" appears verbatim in all 6 Membership sections.

## 3. Useful vs. Harmful Repetition

**Useful (Template Support):**
*   **Structural Consistency:** The "Is this normal?" and "First steps" structures allow users to scan quickly. If a user knows the format, they can skip to "First steps" on any page.
*   **Trust Building:** The consistent "Reviewed by Sally Woods" and "On the consult floor" framing reinforces authority.
*   **Safety Net:** The "When to call your GP" section is a critical, consistent safety feature.

**Harmful (Interchangeable Feeling):**
*   **Verbatim Membership Pitch:** Copy-pasting the entire "All the help, in one place" block on every page creates "banner blindness." Users stop reading it because it offers no new value per page.
*   **Generic "First Steps":** The first step is always "Log for a week." While accurate, it feels like a cop-out. It delays the specific advice the user came for.
*   **FAQ Redundancy (Catnapping):** The FAQ section repeats the "What's going on" and "First steps" content, adding length without new information.
*   **Overwritten Tone:** The "What's going on" sections are dense. They mix mechanism, common traps, and emotional context in one block, making them hard to scan.

## 4. Answer Clarity, Delay, and Job Changes

*   **Clear Answer:** The "Is this normal?" section is the clearest part. It quickly categorizes the user's situation.
*   **Delayed Answer:** The "First steps" section delays the specific fix. Step 1 is always "Log for a week." This is a high-friction first step. The user wants to know *what to do*, not *what to track*. The specific fix is buried in steps 2-4.
*   **Job Change:** The page starts as an **Educational/Reassurance** job ("Is this normal?") and shifts to a **Diagnostic** job ("Log for a week") and then to a **Commercial** job ("Join Membership"). The shift to commercial is abrupt and generic.

## 6. Recommended Shared Page Flow

**Goal:** Reduce cognitive load, increase differentiation, and improve conversion.

**Target Word Budgets:**
*   **Total Page Body:** 600-700 words (excluding nav/footer).
*   **H1 + Lead:** 50 words.
*   **What's Going On:** 100 words.
*   **Is This Normal?:** 100 words.
*   **First Steps:** 150 words.
*   **Free Resource:** 50 words.
*   **Related Help:** 30 words.
*   **Membership Pitch:** 50 words (Condensed, not verbatim).

**New Sequence:**
1.  **H1 + Lead:** Direct answer. "Is this normal? Yes/No. Here is the first check."
2.  **Is This Normal?:** Move this **up**. Users need reassurance before mechanism.
3.  **What's Going On:** Simplify. Remove "On the consult floor" narrative. Focus on mechanism.
4.  **First Steps:** Make Step 1 actionable, not just "Log."
5.  **Free Resource:** Specific, high-value asset.
6.  **Related Help:** Contextual links.
7.  **Membership Pitch:** Condensed. Link to "Which is right for us?" instead of full pitch.

## 8. Cross-Link Recommendations

*   **Challenge to Age:** Each challenge page should link to the most relevant age page (e.g., Catnapping -> 3-4 Months; Bedtime Battles -> Toddler).
*   **Challenge to Challenge:** Keep "Related sleep help" but make it contextual. E.g., Early Rising should link to Catnapping and Nap Transitions, not just generic links.
*   **Commercial Routes:**
    *   **Membership:** Default. Use a condensed pitch on all pages.
    *   **Camp Snooze:** Link from "When to get help" sections for high-stress cases (e.g., Bedtime Battles, Night Wakings).
    *   **Consultations:** Link from "When to call your GP" sections for medical concerns, and from "Worth working on" for complex cases.
    *   **Which is right for us?:** Link from the Membership pitch on all pages.

## 9. Claims Requiring Human/Clinical Review

*   **Fever Threshold:** "Fever over 38C" is mentioned in Early Rising, Night Wakings, and Sleep Regressions. This is a medical threshold. Ensure it aligns with current pediatric guidelines (e.g., NHS, AAP).
*   **Feeding Advice:** "Follow your baby's lead on feeds" in Night Wakings. Ensure this does not contradict exclusive breastfeeding recommendations for infants under 6 months.
*   **Sleep Pressure:** "Test an earlier bedtime" in Early Rising. Ensure this is framed as a trial, not a permanent change, to avoid overtiredness.
*   **Regression Definition:** "4 month change is permanent." Ensure this is clearly distinguished from "sleep training" to avoid misinterpretation.
*   **GP Referral:** "When to call your GP" sections should be reviewed by a medical professional to ensure they do not delay necessary care.
