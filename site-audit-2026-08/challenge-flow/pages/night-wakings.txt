# Night wakings: why your baby wakes overnight and what to do first

Most night waking is your baby surfacing between sleep cycles and looking for whatever got them to sleep, so the fix is usually falling asleep more independently at bedtime, not a longer night feed. Some waking is normal at every age, and newborns wake to feed. Look at bedtime and how your baby settles before anything else.

Reviewed by Sally Woods, The Sleep Concierge &middot updated Jul 2026

## What's going on

Everyone surfaces between sleep cycles, adults included. A baby who has not yet learned to link one cycle to the next wakes fully and looks for whatever got them to sleep in the first place. So the driver is usually how your baby falls asleep at bedtime: a feed, a rock, a hand on the chest, they tend to want it back at every wake. Day timing matters too, because both an overtired and an undertired day fragment the night.

On the consult floor, a common trap is reaching for night weaning or a brand new settling method before looking at bedtime. If your baby falls asleep on a feed or in your arms, that is the piece to work on first, because it is the thing they are looking for at every wake.

## Is this normal?

### Usually normal

- A brief stir that settles itself within a few minutes. Pause before you go in.

- A newborn waking every 2 to 3 hours to feed. Feed responsively and keep nights dark and low-key.

### Worth working on

- Waking fully 30 to 60 minutes after bedtime, or needing the same help back to sleep every time. Look at how your baby falls asleep at bedtime first.

- Wide awake and content for an hour or two in the small hours. Review total day sleep and how early bedtime is.

### When to call your GP

- Waking with fever, pain, refusing feeds or off form. This is not a sleep issue. Check temperature and comfort, and call your GP for a fever over 38C or any signs of illness.

## First steps you can take today

- Log the wakes for a week. Write down each wake time, what you did and how long it took to resettle. The driver shows up in the log in a way that a foggy night memory never does.

- Look at bedtime first. Notice how your baby actually falls asleep. Whatever is in place at the start, a feed, a rock, being held, is usually what they call for at each wake, so this is the piece to work on before anything else.

- Check the day. Add up naps and look at wake windows and bedtime. Both an overtired and an undertired day fragment the night, so the fix might be more day sleep, less day sleep or a small bedtime shift.

- Follow your baby&rsquos lead on feeds. Some night wakes are genuine hunger, especially in younger babies. Follow your baby&rsquos lead and your health advice, and seek guidance if the wakes feel hunger-driven or night feeds are increasing rather than reducing.
