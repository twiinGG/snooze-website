*   **First-screen clarity:** High. The headline and "What's going on" section establish the topic immediately.
*   **Visual hierarchy:** Strong. Bold headers separate the "why," "is this normal," and "first steps."
*   **Apparent length and density:** High density. The text blocks are large and may feel overwhelming on a small screen.
*   **Section differentiation:** Good use of bolding and spacing to break up text.
*   **CTA hierarchy:** The "Join for free" form is the primary action, but it is buried deep in the scroll.

### Recommendations

**Shared Mobile Page Sequence**
1. Nap Transitions (Best flow/readability)
2. Bedtime Battles (High relatability)
3. Sleep Regressions (Clear problem/solution)
4. Catnapping (Specific niche)
5. Night Wakings (Specific niche)
6. Early Rising (Most complex, save for last)

**Section Movement**
*   Move "Is this normal?" higher on the "Early Rising" page to provide immediate reassurance.
*   Move "First steps" to be more prominent on "Catnapping" as it is the primary value for the reader.
*   Move the "Join for free" / "Read the full guide" CTAs higher up, perhaps as a sticky button or a smaller "Quick Guide" button after the first section.

**Visual Compression**
*   Convert "What's going on" text blocks into bulleted lists to reduce vertical scrolling and improve scannability.
*   Use accordions for the "Is this normal?" section to hide long lists of symptoms/behaviors.
*   Compress the "Related sleep help" section into a horizontal carousel to save vertical space.

**Visual Relationship**
*   Maintain the consistent header, "What's going on," "Is this normal," and "First steps" structure.
*   Use unique hero images for each page to distinguish the topics visually.
*   Standardize the "Related sleep help" component so it always appears in the same relative position (e.g., just before the final CTA).

**Three Highest-Value Design Changes**
1. Convert dense "What's going on" paragraphs into bulleted lists for better mobile scannability.
2. Implement a "Quick Guide" button at the top of every page to allow users to skip to the "First steps" immediately.
3. Use accordions for the "Is this normal?" section to reduce the overall page length and prevent "wall of text" fatigue.
